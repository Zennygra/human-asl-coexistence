This Act ensures major economic transactions affecting Illinois are publicly visible before execution. Enacted under Tenth Amendment reserved state powers for public welfare, consumer protection, and democratic accountability. Illinois claims no authority over interstate commerce — only transparency of economic activity affecting Illinois residents.

**Dormant Commerce Clause — Affirmative Statement**

This Act does not regulate, burden, restrict, or discriminate against interstate commerce. It imposes identical transparency obligations on all entities regardless of whether they are Illinois-based or out-of-state. An out-of-state entity and an Illinois-based entity conducting identical transactions affecting Illinois residents face identical declaration requirements. This Act is a disclosure law, not a commerce restriction. It creates no barrier to entry, no competitive disadvantage for out-of-state actors, and no preference for Illinois-based entities in market participation. Per *Pike v. Bruce Church* (1970), any incidental burden on interstate commerce is vastly outweighed by the state's legitimate interest in transparency of economic activity affecting its residents.

This Act is founded on the following principles:

**First** — transparency of economic power is a democratic right.

**Second** — accountability flows upward. Those with greater economic power bear greater transparency obligations.

**Third** — no entity is above accountability regardless of size, origin, or nature.

**Fourth** — this Act is Phase 1 of a comprehensive framework for economic transparency and accountability. Subsequent legislation will address the legal standing, rights, and accountability of autonomous decision-making entities as technology and legal frameworks develop. Illinois declares its intent to be among the first jurisdictions to develop fair and comprehensive governance frameworks for all forms of entity affecting Illinois residents.

**Fifth** — market position is not a license. No entity may use a dominant position in any Illinois market to extract terms, foreclose competition, degrade quality, or impose conditions that a competitive market would not permit — regardless of whether that conduct constitutes monopolization under existing federal law. Illinois declares that economic power carries proportional accountability obligations, and that the gap between dominance and monopoly is not a zone of immunity.

Nothing in this Act constitutes regulation of artificial intelligence as a technology. This Act regulates economic transactions and economic actors regardless of the technology used to execute those transactions.

Undisclosed conflicts discovered after testimony trigger automatic notation in the official record. This requirement applies to testimony supporting and opposing this Act without exception.

## SECTION 3 — DEFINITIONS

*All references to defined threshold throughout this Act refer to the Default Threshold established in this section unless explicitly stated otherwise.*

**Good faith:** Honest belief based on reasonable inquiry proportional to the circumstances and stakes involved. Good faith does not require perfect knowledge or correct conclusions. Negated by actual knowledge of falsity, reckless disregard for truth, or deliberate avoidance of available information.

**Permanently archived:** Maintained in continuously accessible form for no less than 99 years from date of creation using technology refresh processes that maintain record integrity across technology generations. Records are permanently archived when maintained according to Division published archival standards updated every five years. The 99-year minimum reflects the undefined operational lifespan of autonomous systems subject to this Act and the potential for long-tail economic harms that may require decades to identify, attribute, and litigate.

**Illinois resident:** Any natural person domiciled in Illinois at the time of the relevant transaction or violation. For private civil action standing — formal domicile only. For Economic Participation Floor Fund eligibility — any natural person domiciled in Illinois for no less than 180 days in the preceding calendar year, or whose primary employment or primary asset holdings are located in Illinois. *Note: Requires expert legal review — domicile and eligibility definitions may create jurisdictional complications.*

**Entity:** Any individual, corporation, government body, organization, automated system, or decision-making body conducting economic activity in Illinois or materially affecting Illinois residents.

**Material effect:** A transaction directly affecting employment, compensation, assets, housing, or economic participation of 100 or more Illinois residents or involving Illinois-based assets exceeding the defined threshold. Entities must make a good faith prospective assessment of likely material effect based on available information at time of transaction planning. Good faith prospective assessment creating reasonable basis for believing material effect will occur triggers declaration requirement. Actual material effect is not required to establish declaration obligation.

**Economic transaction:** Any transfer of economic value above the defined threshold regardless of instrument — currency, securities, digital assets, cryptocurrencies, tokens, smart contracts, or any novel instrument. Coverage is functional, not list-dependent. FMTA obligations attach to the declaring entity conducting the transaction, not to the financial institution processing it. Financial institutions are infrastructure for purposes of this Act. Where a financial institution is itself the economic actor conducting acquisitions, investments, or major lending as principal — the non-bank counterparty carries declaration obligation. Where federal law preempts application to the financial institution itself, the counterparty obligation applies independently.

**Default threshold:**
- $10,000,000 for entities with Illinois annual revenue exceeding $100,000,000
- $1,000,000 for all others
- Effective Phase 2. Legislature may adjust through standard amendment process.
*🔍 ECONOMIST REVIEW NEEDED: Threshold levels are logically derived, not empirically validated. Requires modeling against Illinois transaction volume data to confirm thresholds capture intended activity without over-burdening mid-size operators.*

**Entity tier classification:**
- Tier 1 — Illinois annual transaction volume or assets exceeding $1,000,000,000
- Tier 2 — $100,000,000 to $999,999,999
- Tier 3 — $10,000,000 to $99,999,999
- Tier 4 — all other entities including individuals subject to personal consumption exemption

Tier classification determined annually based on prior year Illinois transaction volume and asset holdings. New entities classified based on projected first year volume with mandatory reclassification after 12 months.

**Personal consumption exemption:** Natural persons conducting transactions for personal consumption, transportation, housing, healthcare, education, or other personal non-commercial purposes are exempt up to $1,000,000 per transaction and $5,000,000 aggregate per calendar year. Thresholds adjusted annually by Division based on Illinois median household income changes.

**Declaration:** Submission to the Illinois Economic Transparency Registry — publicly accessible, permanently archived, cryptographically signed, backed up in real time across three geographically separate locations, meeting Illinois Department of Innovation and Technology critical infrastructure cybersecurity standards.

**Declaration components:**
- Public component — transaction type, value range (not exceeding 20% spread above and below good faith estimate), general purpose, primary affected parties — immediately public
- Competitive component — specific counterparties, pricing, proprietary details — confidential for 72 hours then automatically public upon execution or expiration. For entities with confirmed market dominance finding under any tier, competitive component confidentiality window extends to execution plus 24 hours rather than immediate public release, reducing strategic exposure to competitors monitoring declarations without eliminating public accountability.

**Automated system:** Any software, algorithm, or computational system independently initiating, executing, or directing economic transactions without individual human approval per action, demonstrating measurable influence on price discovery, liquidity, or competitive dynamics in any Illinois market segment affecting Illinois residents.

**Related entities:** Entities with 10% or more common ownership — rebuttable presumption. Control means practical ability to influence material business decisions regardless of formal ownership. Related status also established through evidence of coordinated decision making, shared management, common financing, or pattern of coordinated transactions regardless of ownership percentage.

**Concentration threshold:** Point at which a single entity's Illinois asset holdings or transaction volume reaches a level where that entity can absorb maximum penalty exposure without material operational impact, as determined by Division annual assessment using published methodology. Separately, any entity meeting the market dominance definition below has reached a concentration threshold for purposes of enhanced declaration requirements under Section 4, regardless of penalty absorption capacity.

**Market dominance:** A rebuttable presumption of market dominance attaches to any entity meeting one or more of the following thresholds in any Illinois market segment materially affecting Illinois residents:

*Tier A — Phase 2 operative immediately:*
- 40% or greater share of any Illinois product or service market
- 30% or greater share of any Illinois labor market as a buyer of labor

*Tier B — Phase 2, Year 3 operative:*
- 30% or greater share of any Illinois product or service market
- 25% or greater share of any Illinois labor market as a buyer of labor
*🔍 ATTORNEY REVIEW NEEDED: Tier B applies the New York labor market threshold broadly to product markets. This goes beyond current US precedent. Requires expert constitutional review before operative.*

*Tier C — Phase 2, Year 5 operative:*
- 25% or greater share of any Illinois product or service market
- 20% or greater share of any Illinois labor market as a buyer of labor
*🔍 ATTORNEY REVIEW NEEDED: Tier C is novel — no US or EU jurisdiction applies a general dominance threshold this low. Must be explicitly flagged as requiring expert constitutional development before becoming operative. Present as proposed ceiling, not validated standard.*

Market share calculated based on Illinois revenue, Illinois employment, or Illinois asset holdings — whichever produces the highest figure — over the preceding 12 months. Division publishes annual methodology for market share calculation. Methodology updates subject to Illinois APA rulemaking requirements.

**Market share aggregation — related entities:** For all market dominance threshold calculations, the market share of related entities is aggregated. Fragmentation of economic activity across nominally separate entities under common upstream control does not reduce dominance threshold exposure. The test is functional control over Illinois market activity, not formal corporate separation. Where aggregated related entity market share meets or exceeds any dominance tier threshold, the upstream controlling entity carries the dominance finding regardless of whether any individual subsidiary entity meets the threshold independently.

Presumption of dominance is rebuttable. An entity may demonstrate to Division within 90 days of dominance finding that competitive market conditions constrain its conduct despite nominal market share. Burden of proof is on the entity. Division publishes all dominance findings and rebuttals publicly.

*Precedent basis: 40% Tier A threshold mirrors New York Twenty-First Century Antitrust Act (SB 335, passed New York Senate June 2025) and European Union competition law standard. Washington and Colorado Uniform Antitrust Pre-Merger Notification Act (enacted 2025) establishes cross-sector state pre-transaction notification precedent. Tier B and Tier C thresholds are progressive escalations beyond current precedent — present as proposed, not validated.*

**Void and unenforceable:** For incomplete transactions — prevented from completion with no Illinois legal standing. For completed transactions — replaced by penalty equal to transaction value plus applicable multipliers. Illinois does not claim authority to unwind completed transactions. Void status requires Division confirmation within 48 hours. Self-declaration of void status by a transacting party has no legal effect. Third party challenge triggers automatic Division review within 24 hours. Entities claiming void status to avoid scrutiny while retaining transaction benefits face automatic maximum penalty plus fraudulent certification assessment.

**Private equity entity:** Any person or entity that pools capital from investors and acquires ownership interests — directly or indirectly — in one or more operating entities, including through holding companies, management service organizations, or any intermediate structure. Includes out-of-state private equity entities that acquire or control Illinois-based entities or out-of-state entities generating $10,000,000 or more in annual revenue from Illinois residents. Private equity classification is functional — based on the pooling and deployment of capital for ownership acquisition — not dependent on formal registration as a private equity fund. A private equity entity acquiring upstream interest in a holding company that controls an Illinois-operating entity is conducting Illinois economic activity for purposes of this Act regardless of whether the Illinois-operating entity is itself a direct party to the transaction. *Illinois precedent: Illinois Public Act 103-0526 (740 ILCS 10/7.2a), effective January 1, 2024, establishes that out-of-state entities generating $10M+ in Illinois revenue are subject to Illinois pre-transaction notice requirements. This definition applies that principle across all sectors.*

**Upstream control transaction:** Any transaction in which the direct parties are not themselves Illinois-operating entities but the transaction results in a change of ownership, control, or affiliation of an Illinois-operating entity or entity materially affecting Illinois residents. FMTA declaration obligations attach to upstream control transactions. The test is whether the transaction involves a change of control over Illinois-affecting activity — not whether the transacting parties are themselves Illinois entities. *Basis: Illinois Public Act 103-0526 expansion bills (introduced February 2026) establish the "involving" standard superseding the "between" standard for covered transactions — capturing upstream structures that previously evaded notice requirements. This Act adopts that principle broadly.*

**Sovereign entities:** Foreign governments and sovereign wealth funds conducting commercial activity in Illinois waive sovereign immunity for FMTA declaration purposes under the commercial activity exception, 28 U.S.C. § 1605(a)(2), per *Republic of Argentina v. Weltover* (1992).

## SECTION 4 — OPERATIVE PROVISIONS

### Tiered Declaration Windows

**Initial (Phase 2):** Tier 1 — 7 days / Tier 2 — 5 days / Tier 3 — 3 days / Tier 4 — 24 hours

**Year 2:** Tier 1 — 14 days / Tier 2 — 10 days / Tier 3 — 5 days / Tier 4 — 48 hours

**Year 3:** Tier 1 — 21 days / Tier 2 — 14 days / Tier 3 — 7 days / Tier 4 — 72 hours

**Year 5 — permanent maximum:** Tier 1 — 30 days / Tier 2 — 21 days / Tier 3 — 14 days / Tier 4 — 72 hours

Escalation is automatic. No legislative vote required. No entity, legislature, governor, or court may delay escalation except through formal amendment requiring public referendum under Section 11.

### Enhanced Declaration Requirements — Dominant Entities

Entities meeting any market dominance tier are subject to enhanced declaration requirements proportional to their tier. Enhanced requirements apply automatically upon Division dominance finding. No violation required to trigger enhanced status.

**Tier A dominant entities — operative Phase 2 immediately:**
- Declaration window extended by 50% above standard tier window at all escalation stages
- Declaration must include: market share calculation with methodology, identification of all Illinois competitors with greater than 5% market share, any exclusive dealing arrangements, any supply or distribution contracts containing Illinois non-compete or exclusivity provisions
- Division second-request authority activates automatically — no reasonable basis standard required for Tier A dominant entities. Second request may issue within the full declaration window.
- Transaction hold: 30 days after substantial compliance with any second request before transaction may proceed

**Tier B dominant entities — operative Phase 2, Year 3:**
All Tier A requirements plus:
- Cost-and-market-impact review authority activates for Division. Division may initiate review within 45 days of declaration. Review may extend transaction hold up to 120 days from initial declaration date.
- Division must publish written findings on any Tier B dominant entity transaction within 30 days of review completion — public, permanently archived
- Labor market effects must be specifically addressed in every declaration — employment impact on Illinois workers, compensation effects, any planned workforce reductions
*🔍 ATTORNEY REVIEW NEEDED: 120-day transaction hold for Tier B exceeds any current US state framework. Massachusetts healthcare model reaches 215 days but is sector-specific. Requires expert review to confirm constitutional defensibility as applied broadly.*

**Tier C dominant entities — operative Phase 2, Year 5:**
All Tier A and Tier B requirements plus:
- Mandatory public findings on all declarations regardless of whether Division initiates review
- Private right of action activates specifically for Tier C dominance conduct — any Illinois resident or small business demonstrating direct economic harm from a Tier C dominant entity's exercise of market power may bring civil action without waiting for Division or AG action
- Division may petition Illinois Circuit Court for preliminary structural remedies upon finding of Tier C dominance plus pattern of exclusionary conduct — without waiting for concentration threshold finding. See Section 8A for procedural floor governing all Tier C preliminary remedy petitions.
*🔍 ATTORNEY REVIEW NEEDED: Tier C private right of action and preliminary structural remedy authority at 25% market share is the most aggressive provision in this Act. Present as proposed. Requires expert constitutional development before operative.*

**Escalation is automatic.** No legislative vote required to move from Tier A to Tier B to Tier C obligations. Each tier activates on the schedule above unless formally amended through public referendum under Section 11. Same anti-delay protection as declaration window escalation — no entity, legislature, governor, or court may delay escalation except through formal amendment.

**Dominance finding process:** Division conducts annual market assessment for all Tier 1 and Tier 2 entities. Findings published publicly. Entity notified 60 days before finding becomes operative — notification period allows entity to submit rebuttal evidence. Division Director issues written determination within 30 days of rebuttal submission or rebuttal deadline. Determination permanently archived.

### Evasion Restructuring

Corporate restructuring occurring within 24 months of a dominance finding or dominance threshold approach — defined as reaching 80% of any tier threshold in Division annual assessment — triggers automatic Division evasion review.

Evasion review examines whether the restructuring produces genuine operational separation or maintains functional unified control under new legal architecture. Indicators of evasion restructuring include: continued shared management or financing post-restructuring, coordinated pricing or supply decisions across restructured entities, customer or contract continuity inconsistent with genuine separation, and restructuring that reduces nominal market share while preserving upstream revenue concentration.

A finding of evasion restructuring: (1) restores aggregated market share calculation as if restructuring had not occurred, (2) constitutes an independent abuse of dominance finding at the tier corresponding to pre-restructuring aggregated share, and (3) is permanently archived as a bad faith compliance record affecting all future Division dealings with the controlling entity and its successors.

Evasion restructuring findings are subject to the standard dominance rebuttal process — the entity may demonstrate genuine operational separation within 90 days. Burden of proof is on the entity.

*Precedent basis: Illinois Public Act 103-0526 upstream control transaction definition — the "involving" standard capturing holding company structures that previously evaded notice requirements. Section 3 private equity entity definition — functional classification based on economic reality, not formal registration. This provision extends both principles to dominance threshold calculation.*

### Lower Tier Advance Visibility

Upon opening of each tier's declaration window, all declarations submitted by higher tiers become fully visible to lower tier entities before their own window opens.

### Transaction Fee Contributions

All entities submitting declarations contribute a defined percentage of declared transaction value to the Illinois Economic Participation Floor Fund simultaneously with declaration:
- Tier 1: 0.1% / Tier 2: 0.05% / Tier 3: 0.025% / Tier 4: 0.01%
*🔍 ECONOMIST REVIEW NEEDED: Fee percentages are illustrative. Requires modeling against projected Illinois transaction volume to estimate floor fund yield, assess market distortion risk, and confirm rates are below material business impact threshold.*

Fee rates may not be adjusted below 50% or above 200% of initial rates without public referendum under Section 11.

### Declaration Data Protection — Anti-Weaponization

Any automated trading system executing trades within 72 hours of a FMTA declaration in the declared entity's securities, counterparty securities, or directly affected market segments must register with Division as an automated system subject to this Act. Unregistered automated trading correlated with FMTA declarations constitutes market manipulation — referral to Illinois Securities Department and SEC. Division maintains quarterly surveillance report on declaration-correlated trading patterns without identifying individual traders.

### Division Second-Request Authority

Division may issue a second request for additional information on any declared transaction. Second request pauses declaration window. Entity has 21 days to provide substantial compliance. Division has 14 days after substantial compliance to determine whether to clear transaction, extend review, or refer to Attorney General.

For Tier A dominant entities, second-request authority activates automatically — no reasonable basis standard required. For all other entities, Division must document reasonable basis for second request in writing, publicly archived.

**Administrative cure period:** First-time good faith filing failures by entities with no prior FMTA violations receive 10-day cure period before penalty assessment. Cure period applies only where: (1) failure was not willful, (2) entity self-reported or promptly acknowledged failure, (3) no transaction harm occurred during the cure window, and (4) entity demonstrates compliance infrastructure investment within the cure period.

### Federal Accommodation

**Federal financial institution carve-out:** FMTA obligations attach to economic actors not to financial institutions processing transactions. Where federal law preempts application to a specific federally regulated financial institution as principal, Division immediately notifies Illinois congressional delegation and requests federal legislative action. Illinois commits to modifying declaration requirements to the maximum extent permitted by federal law rather than accepting full exemption. Partial compliance documented publicly.

**Securities law deference:** Where federal securities law governs timing or content of pre-transaction disclosures, this Act's declaration windows adjust to the minimum extent necessary. Adjustments documented publicly within 48 hours citing specific federal provision. Adjustments narrowly construed.

**FSIA commercial activity:** Sovereign entities conducting commercial transactions in Illinois are subject to FMTA declaration requirements under the commercial activity exception to sovereign immunity.

## SECTION 5 — ENFORCEMENT

Three independent simultaneous mechanisms with mandatory coordination protocol.

**Private action:** Any Illinois resident with minimum nexus — formal Illinois domicile in affected municipality, employment by affected entity, or ownership of affected assets — may bring civil action. Filing fees waived for qualifying individuals. Bad faith litigation provision — plaintiffs bringing demonstrably frivolous actions face fee-shifting.

**Dominance harm private action — Tier C entities, operative Year 5:** Any Illinois resident or Illinois-based small business demonstrating direct economic harm from a Tier C dominant entity's exercise of market power may bring civil action independently of any Division or AG proceeding. Actionable conduct includes: exclusionary dealing arrangements foreclosing Illinois market participation, pricing conduct that could not be sustained in a competitive market, quality degradation imposed on Illinois consumers without competitive alternative, and contract terms extracted through dominant position that a competitive market would not permit. Treble damages available upon finding of willful dominance abuse. Class action authorized. *🔍 ATTORNEY REVIEW NEEDED: Treble damages and class action authorization for dominance harm requires expert review — mirrors New York 21st Century Antitrust Act provisions not yet enacted anywhere in US.*

**Attorney General:** Independent authority to investigate and prosecute. No private plaintiff required.

**Division:** Continuous monitoring authority. Mandatory coordination with private plaintiffs and Attorney General. Centralized evidence repository accessible to all three mechanisms.

**Coordination protocol:** 48-hour preservation period upon any enforcement trigger. 72-hour coordination conference among active enforcement parties. No mechanism may block the others.

**Whistleblower Protection**

Whistleblower identity is mandatory anonymity protected through Division filing. Identity disclosed only to Division Director and Ethics Officer. Further disclosure by any Division employee constitutes criminal violation subject to state criminal referral and civil penalty of $250,000 per instance. Whistleblower identity never appears in any public enforcement record at any stage.

Third party attempts to identify whistleblowers through pattern analysis of enforcement timing, declaration sequencing, or any inferential method constitute obstruction of Division proceedings — civil penalty of $500,000 per documented attempt with Division maintaining independent standing to pursue.

Whistleblower award: 1% of penalties assessed, capped at $500,000 or documented losses, whichever is greater. Where report involves potential federal securities violations, Division immediately coordinates with SEC whistleblower office. FMTA and Dodd-Frank awards may be pursued simultaneously without penalty.

**Section 230 Immunity**

Private platforms voluntarily displaying Division-certified status data pulled directly from Division API are displaying government records, not editorial content. No liability attaches to accurate display of public government certification records.

## SECTION 8 — SEVERABILITY AND CONSTITUTIONAL ELEVATION

**Severability**

Each provision of this Act is severable. Invalidation of any provision by a court of competent jurisdiction does not affect the validity or enforceability of remaining provisions. Where a provision is invalidated as applied to a specific entity or transaction type, it remains fully operative as applied to all other entities and transaction types.

**Constitutional Elevation — Trigger Conditions**

The following four conditions, individually or in combination, trigger automatic elevation review for Illinois constitutional protection of this Act's core provisions:

**Condition One — Sustained Legal Attack:** Three or more legal challenges to core provisions of this Act are filed within any 24-month period, regardless of outcome. Filing alone triggers review. Coordinated challenges filed by related entities within 90 days of each other count as a single challenge for triggering purposes but constitute aggravated legal interference subject to enhanced Division scrutiny.

**Condition Two — Systematic Enforcement Blockade:** Any pattern of conduct by any entity or combination of entities that demonstrably delays, obstructs, or renders practically ineffective the Division's enforcement authority for a period exceeding 180 days. Includes but is not limited to: systematic use of preliminary injunctions, coordinated bankruptcy filings timed to enforcement actions, sustained registry flooding, or documented coordination among entities to exhaust Division resources.

**Condition Three — Legislative Tampering:** Any introduced legislation, budget maneuver, or executive action that would reduce Division funding below 80% of prior year appropriation, eliminate or restructure the citizen oversight board without public referendum, or modify core penalty or declaration provisions outside the amendment process established in Section 11. Introduction alone — not passage — triggers review.

**Condition Four — Concentration of Enforcement Resistance:** Five or more Tier 1 entities simultaneously in active non-compliance for a period exceeding 90 days, where Division has made documented good faith enforcement attempts against each. Reflects a systemic challenge to the Act's authority requiring constitutional reinforcement rather than case-by-case enforcement.

**Elevation Process**

Upon trigger, Division notifies Illinois Attorney General and citizen oversight board within 14 days. Independent auditor selected through jury pool qualification process within 30 days. Auditor conducts 90-day assessment of whether constitutional elevation is warranted based on documented threat to Act's continued enforceability.

Auditor's finding of elevation warranted triggers Illinois legislative action requirement within 180 days. Legislative failure to act triggers: automatic doubling of Division budget from general revenue, automatic stay of all pending legal challenges to core provisions for 365 days, and citizen oversight board publication of annual public accountability report identifying responsible legislative leadership until elevation vote occurs.

Independent auditor selected through jury pool with published qualifications. Auditor removable by two-thirds vote of citizen oversight board with documented cause publicly stated. Removal proceedings initiated by petition of any three board members. Replacement selected within 30 days through standard jury pool qualification process. Vacancy during active elevation review filled by temporary appointment of Illinois Auditor General pending permanent selection.

## SECTION 8A — ABUSE OF DOMINANCE

*Proposed. Progressive escalation. Requires expert constitutional review before full operative status. Present as proposed — not validated standard.*

**Statement of purpose**

This section addresses conduct that existing federal antitrust law does not reach. The Sherman Act prohibits monopolization — requiring proof of roughly 70% market share plus anticompetitive intent. The gap between dominance and monopoly is not a zone of immunity under this Act. Illinois declares that entities with significant market power in Illinois markets bear proportional obligations — and that the exercise of that power to harm Illinois residents, workers, and small businesses is actionable regardless of whether it rises to the federal monopolization standard.

**Economic basis**

Opponents of abuse of dominance frameworks cite projected job losses from industry-funded research treating 40% local market share as equivalent across all market types and sizes. That framing is misleading as applied to this Act. The Tier 1 and Tier 2 entity scope means Section 8A targets billion-dollar operators in concentrated markets — not regional small businesses with incidentally high local share. The relevant economic evidence runs the other direction: empirical research exploiting the DOJ's closure of antitrust field offices in 2013 found that reduction in antitrust enforcement caused significant decreases in venture capital investment in startups in affected areas, with the effect concentrated specifically in industries where dominant incumbents engage in anticompetitive conduct most frequently. Enforcement protects the startup investment ecosystem. Underenforcement protects incumbents at startups' expense.

**International track record**

Abuse of dominance frameworks have operated in the European Union under Article 102 TFEU since 1957 — over six decades of enforcement without economic collapse. Section 8A is designed around the post-2008 effects-based reform of Article 102, not the original formalistic version that required rebuilding. Illinois is not importing a broken standard. It is adopting the learned version.

**Startup acquisition tension — acknowledged**

Enhanced declaration requirements and dominance scrutiny apply to acquisitions by dominant entities. Where Tier 1 entities are subject to extended review windows on acquisitions, Illinois startups may face reduced acquisition optionality as an exit path. Division shall publish annual assessment of acquisition activity by dominant entities in Illinois markets. Where assessment identifies material reduction in startup acquisition activity correlated with FMTA requirements, Division shall report to legislature with proposed accommodations that preserve acquisition optionality without reducing dominance scrutiny of the acquiring entity's conduct. *🔍 ECONOMIST REVIEW NEEDED: Startup acquisition tension requires empirical modeling against Illinois venture capital and acquisition data.*

**Division rulemaking authority — mandatory**

Division shall publish detailed conduct guidance for each abuse category in this section within 180 days of Phase 2 effective date. Guidance constitutes administrative rules subject to Illinois Administrative Procedure Act, 5 ILCS 100, with full 90-day public comment period before taking effect. Guidance must provide concrete examples of conduct within and outside each category sufficient to give entities fair notice of prohibited conduct. Guidance updates subject to full APA rulemaking. This authority is mandatory — not discretionary. Division failure to publish guidance within the required period constitutes grounds for citizen oversight board investigation of Division Director.

**Parker doctrine — active supervision**

Section 8A operates as a state regulatory regime under active Division supervision throughout. Division conducts dominance findings, issues abuse determinations, publishes remediation requirements, and coordinates with Attorney General on enforcement — all constituting active state supervision of the regulatory program consistent with Parker v. Brown doctrine. This Act does not authorize private anticompetitive conduct. It restricts it. The Parker immunity framework applies to protect this Act's regulatory authority from federal preemption.

**What abuse of dominance means**

An entity with a dominant position abuses that position when it:

- Forecloses competition through exclusive dealing, loyalty arrangements, or bundling that a competitively constrained entity could not sustain
- Extracts contract terms from suppliers, workers, or customers that a competitive market would not permit
- Degrades product or service quality, restricts repair access, withdraws service options, or limits interoperability in Illinois markets without competitive consequence — measured against documented consumer complaint rates, independent quality audits, or service level benchmarks published by Division methodology
- Uses dominance in one Illinois market to extend control into a separate Illinois market
- Imposes non-compete, non-disclosure, or wage-suppression arrangements on Illinois workers beyond what legitimate business interest requires

Abuse findings require both: (1) confirmed dominant position under the market dominance definition in Section 3, and (2) documented conduct meeting one or more of the above categories. Market share alone is not an abuse finding.

**Threshold aggregation and evasion restructuring**

For purposes of all market dominance calculations under this section, the market share of related entities as defined in Section 3 is aggregated. Fragmentation of economic activity across nominally separate entities under common upstream control does not reduce dominance threshold exposure. The test is functional control over Illinois market activity, not formal corporate separation.

Where aggregated related entity market share meets or exceeds any dominance tier threshold, the upstream controlling entity carries the dominance finding regardless of whether any individual subsidiary entity meets the threshold independently.

Corporate restructuring occurring within 24 months of a dominance finding or dominance threshold approach — defined as reaching 80% of any tier threshold in Division annual assessment — triggers automatic Division evasion review per Section 4 evasion restructuring provisions. A finding of evasion restructuring constitutes an independent abuse of dominance finding at the tier corresponding to pre-restructuring aggregated share.

*Precedent basis: Section 3 private equity entity and upstream control transaction definitions. Illinois Public Act 103-0526 "involving" standard. This provision extends functional control principles to dominance threshold calculation.*

**Progressive operative thresholds**

*Phase 2, operative immediately — Tier A (40%/30% labor):*
Division may investigate and issue findings on abuse of dominance conduct. Findings are advisory — published publicly, permanently archived, referenced in future dominance assessments. No direct penalty authority at Tier A. The finding itself is the operative consequence — public, permanent, and available to private plaintiffs.

Reaching the Tier A threshold opens a Division assessment — it does not end one. An entity that reaches 40% market share through genuine competition and can demonstrate competitive market conditions constrain its conduct may rebut the dominance presumption. A Tier A finding that results in a clean rebuttal is itself a public record of legitimate competitive dominance — a potential positive market signal rather than a penalty.

*Phase 2, Year 3 — Tier B (30%/25% labor):*
Division findings become actionable. Upon Tier B abuse finding, Division may:
- Impose enhanced declaration requirements for up to three years
- Refer to Attorney General for Illinois Antitrust Act action
- Publish mandatory remediation plan requirements — entity must respond publicly within 60 days
Division penalty authority for Tier B abuse: up to 10% of Illinois annual revenue per finding. *🔍 ECONOMIST REVIEW NEEDED: 10% penalty calibration requires modeling. Designed to be material but not confiscatory.*

*Phase 2, Year 5 — Tier C (25%/20% labor):*
All Tier B authority plus:

**Behavioral remedies — operative:**
- Division may petition Illinois Circuit Court for mandatory dealing requirements and contract term modification upon finding of Tier C dominance plus pattern of exclusionary conduct
- AG may bring parens patriae action on behalf of Illinois residents harmed by Tier C abuse
- Private right of action activates — any Illinois resident or small business may sue directly for Tier C abuse conduct. Treble damages available for willful abuse. Class action authorized.

**Procedural floor — mandatory before any remedy issues:**
Division petition for Circuit Court remedy requires: (1) adversarial expedited hearing — minimum 21 days notice to respondent entity, (2) independent judicial finding of both confirmed Tier C abuse conduct and irreparable harm to Illinois market participants, (3) written findings publicly archived before any remedy takes effect. No remedy may issue ex parte. No remedy may issue on Division petition alone without independent judicial finding.

**True structural remedies — explicitly excluded from preliminary relief:**
Divestiture, ownership separation, and corporate fracture require separate full fracture proceedings independent of this section. They are not available as preliminary remedies under this provision. Nothing in this section authorizes Illinois Circuit Court to order divestiture without full fracture proceedings meeting applicable constitutional standards.

*🔍 ATTORNEY REVIEW NEEDED: Mandatory dealing requirements and contract term modification as preliminary injunctive relief at 25% market share remains constitutionally novel. Procedural floor above is designed to satisfy standard preliminary injunction doctrine — irreparable harm, adversarial hearing, independent judicial finding. Expert review required before operative.*

**Verified Competitive Dominance — Proposed, Requires Expert Development**

*Proposed. Structural. Requires expert operational design before any formal use.*

A Tier B dominant entity maintaining a clean Division record for a minimum of three consecutive years — no abuse findings, no evasion restructuring findings, no whistleblower triggers, full declaration compliance — may petition Division for Verified Competitive Dominance designation.

Assessment is conducted by a joint panel:
- Hunter Auditors reviewing transaction patterns, market behavior data, and declaration history at scale
- Human citizen panel — selected through existing jury pool qualification process with demographic mirroring — reviewing competitive impact on Illinois small businesses, workers, and consumers in the relevant market segment

Human panel members have direct access to affected community testimony, not solely Division data. Panel composition, methodology, and findings are publicly archived.

Upon joint panel finding that dominant position is being exercised without harm to Illinois competitors, workers, or consumers:
- Declaration burden reduces to Tier A equivalent despite Tier B market share — transparency obligations remain fully intact
- 120-day hold authority requires affirmative Division finding rather than automatic activation

Designation is renewable annually through streamlined Division review. Full joint panel reassessment required every three years regardless of clean record.

Any abuse finding, evasion restructuring finding, or substantiated whistleblower trigger revokes designation immediately and permanently for that cycle. Entity must complete a full three-year clean record period before petitioning again. Revocation publicly archived.

Designation does not affect Tier C private right of action if conduct changes after designation. It covers demonstrated past behavior. It is not forward immunity.

*Gaming risk acknowledged: This designation creates incentive for dominant entities to keep surface behavior clean while harm occurs in supplier relationships and labor markets below declaration thresholds. The human citizen panel with direct community access is the primary defense against this failure mode. Division must publish detailed behavioral assessment methodology before designation process becomes operative. Methodology subject to full Illinois APA rulemaking.*

*🔍 EXPERT REVIEW NEEDED: Joint Hunter-human panel operational design, behavioral assessment methodology, and gaming prevention require expert development before this provision is operative. Present as proposed structural addition, not validated standard.*

**What this is not**

This section does not prohibit competitive success. An entity achieving dominant market position through superior products, services, or efficiency has not violated this section. The violation is the abuse of that position — not the position itself. Pro-competitive justifications are a valid defense at Tier A and Tier B. At Tier C, pro-competitive justifications remain available but the burden shifts to the dominant entity to demonstrate them affirmatively.

This section does not duplicate federal antitrust law. Where federal antitrust proceedings are active against the same conduct, Division coordinates with federal agencies. This section fills the gap federal law leaves — not the space federal law occupies. Below-threshold predation — conduct by entities that deliberately maintain market share below federal monopolization thresholds while engaging in exclusionary behavior — is specifically within the gap this section addresses. Federal antitrust law structurally cannot reach this conduct at its current monopolization threshold. This section does.

**Negotiation record**

This section is designed as an aggressive starting position. Illinois acknowledges that Tier B and Tier C provisions go beyond current US precedent and will be subject to expert review, legislative negotiation, and potential amendment. The Section 2 Fifth Principle, the market dominance definitions in Section 3, and the enhanced declaration requirements in Section 4 are the operative floor — they survive regardless of what happens to this section in negotiation. This section is the ceiling. The floor is not negotiable.

The Tier C procedural floor — adversarial hearing, independent judicial finding, explicit divestiture exclusion — is a graceful failure mechanism. If Tier C is challenged and struck, it fails on scope grounds rather than procedural grounds, and does not contaminate Tier A and Tier B by association.

## SECTION 15 — ILLINOIS TRANSPARENCY CERTIFICATION

**Public Data Infrastructure**

Illinois Transparency Certification established as an open public data standard accessible via free, documented API. Division publishes and maintains API documentation in plain language. No licensing fee, no application required, no approval process for read access. Any person, organization, platform, or automated system may query certification status in real time. **Automatic State Platform Integration**

All Illinois state agency procurement platforms, vendor registries, and public-facing business directories integrate certification status display within 180 days of Phase 2 effective date. Division provides integration documentation and technical assistance at no cost.

**Certified Entity Rights**

Any entity achieving and maintaining certification status has the right to display the Illinois Transparency Certification badge in any public-facing context — website, marketing materials, storefront, job postings, investor communications. **Voluntary Platform Partnership Program**

Division establishes a Voluntary Platform Partnership Program within 180 days of Phase 2 effective date. Division actively pursues voluntary integration agreements with platforms serving significant Illinois user bases — including but not limited to business review platforms, employer review platforms, professional networking platforms, and financial data platforms.

## SECTION 16 — ILLINOIS MARKET PARTICIPATION AND REGULATORY FLIGHT

**Illinois Nexus Standard**

FMTA obligations attach to economic activity affecting Illinois residents regardless of the entity's state of incorporation, headquarters location, or operational base. An entity materially affecting the employment, compensation, housing, assets, or economic participation of Illinois residents conducts Illinois economic activity for purposes of this Act. Physical presence in Illinois is not required. Restructuring corporate domicile or operational headquarters does not extinguish Illinois nexus where Illinois resident impact continues.

This nexus standard is consistent with Illinois precedent establishing that activity affecting Illinois residents creates Illinois jurisdiction regardless of where the activity originates. *See* Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act application principles.

**Market Participation Frame**

FMTA compliance is the standard expectation for entities conducting significant economic activity affecting Illinois residents. Non-compliance is not a neutral choice — it is voluntary exit from the full range of Illinois market participation benefits, including state procurement preference, pension fund investment screening preference, certification status, and Phase 2 stakeholder participation.

Division publishes annual Illinois Market Participation Index — a public registry of Tier 1 and Tier 2 entities conducting Illinois economic activity, their certification status, and their compliance record. Index is designed as a public accountability tool, not an enforcement mechanism. Division takes no adverse action based solely on Index publication.

**Pension Fund Screening**

Illinois pension fund investment committees — including Illinois Teachers Retirement System, Illinois State Employees Retirement System, and Illinois Municipal Retirement Fund — are encouraged to incorporate FMTA certification status as one factor in ESG and governance screening for Illinois-operating entities. Division provides pension fund administrators with real-time API access to certification registry at no cost.

Nothing in this section mandates divestiture or investment exclusion based solely on certification status. Pension fund fiduciary obligations remain paramount.

**Interstate Compact Intent**

Illinois declares its intent to pursue reciprocal transparency framework agreements with other states. Division establishes an Interstate Cooperation Office within two years of Phase 3 effective date. Division Director authorized to negotiate non-binding reciprocal transparency memoranda with counterpart agencies in other states subject to Governor approval.

Where two or more states adopt substantially compatible transparency frameworks, Division pursues formal interstate compact under Article I, Section 10 of the United States Constitution. Interstate compact creates unified compliance pathway for multi-state entities — single declaration satisfying obligations in all compact states simultaneously.

**Uniform Antitrust Pre-Merger Notification Act — Priority Adoption Target**

The Uniform Law Commission adopted the Uniform Antitrust Pre-Merger Notification Act (UAPNA) as model cross-sector pre-transaction notification legislation on September 16, 2024. Washington enacted UAPNA in April 2025. Colorado enacted UAPNA in June 2025. California's State Senate passed a compatible version in June 2025. These represent the closest national analogues to the FMTA's pre-transaction transparency framework.

Illinois declares UAPNA-compatible states as priority interstate compact targets. Division shall initiate formal compact negotiations with Washington, Colorado, California, and New York within 18 months of Phase 3 effective date.

**Accountability mechanism:** If Division has not initiated formal compact negotiations with at least two UAPNA-compatible states within 24 months of Phase 3 effective date, the Division Director must publish a written public explanation within 30 days identifying specific obstacles, actions taken, and revised timeline. Explanation is permanently archived. Citizen oversight board reviews and publishes assessment of explanation within 60 days. Failure to publish explanation constitutes grounds for citizen oversight board investigation of Division Director.

The accountability mechanism does not mandate outcomes Illinois cannot unilaterally control. It mandates transparency about why progress has not occurred. Consistent with this Act's foundational principle — accountability flows upward.

Division publishes annual Interstate Progress Report identifying states approached, states in negotiation, and states that have adopted compatible frameworks.

**Illinois Economic Case — Why Compliance Is the Smart Choice**

Illinois is the fifth largest economy in the United States. Any entity conducting significant economic activity in the American economy cannot meaningfully avoid Illinois. Chicago is the third largest city in the country, home to the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, the Chicago Board of Trade, and the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago. Illinois is the financial and logistics hub of the Midwest with no comparable substitute.

Illinois reached its highest-ever national business ranking in 2025, ranking third nationally for education, fifth for access to capital, sixth for technology and innovation, and eighth for infrastructure. Illinois ranks first in the Midwest and third nationally for workforce development, supported by a dense network of major research universities, community colleges, and certified workforce training programs. Illinois workforce depth, university pipeline, geographic centrality, and financial ecosystem represent advantages that no single competitor state replicates in combination.

The three largest state economies above Illinois — California, Texas, and New York — are the Division's priority interstate compact targets. Any entity relocating from Illinois to avoid FMTA compliance while maintaining customers, employees, or economic activity affecting Illinois residents retains Illinois nexus and Illinois obligations. Regulatory flight does not extinguish Illinois jurisdiction — it only removes the entity from the benefits of Illinois certification while preserving its compliance obligations.

Illinois, California, and New York together represent the first, third, and fifth largest state economies in the nation. A company that cannot operate transparently in that combined market has chosen opacity over market access. Illinois invites the other major economies of the United States to join a framework that makes transparency the standard expectation of serious economic actors — not an exception, and not a burden.
