# FMTA — INTENTIONALLY SACRIFICIAL PROVISIONS
## Aggressive Opening Positions Designed for Negotiation
### Not the floor. The ceiling. The floor is not negotiable.

---

**Version:** v1-1 — Instance 16: Threshold aggregation and evasion restructuring moved to floor. Verified Competitive Dominance designation added as proactive concession offering. Tier C graceful failure mechanism noted.

**Purpose of this document**

These provisions are deliberately aggressive. They are designed to give opponents something to negotiate against — so that when they succeed in removing or weakening them, the core floor provisions survive untouched. Every provision here is honest, legally grounded to some degree, and worth fighting for. But if forced to choose, these are what you trade.

The floor — Section 2 Fifth Principle, Section 3 market dominance definitions, Section 4 enhanced declaration requirements — is not listed here. That survives everything.

---

## TIER 1 — TRADE FIRST
*Highest profile. Most legally novel. Most likely to draw first challenge. Give these up early to protect everything else.*

**Section 8A — Tier C Abuse of Dominance (25%/20% labor threshold)**
- Preliminary behavioral remedies at 25% market share — mandatory dealing and contract term modification
- Private right of action with treble damages and class action at Tier C
- AG parens patriae action for Tier C conduct
- No US precedent. Novel even by EU standards at this threshold. Designed as the ceiling. Trade it for Tier A and Tier B survival.
- *Note: Tier C now has procedural floor — adversarial hearing, independent judicial finding, explicit divestiture exclusion. This is a graceful failure mechanism, not a hardening. If Tier C is challenged and struck, it fails on scope grounds not procedural grounds, protecting Tier A and Tier B from contamination by association.*

**Section 8A — Tier B Abuse of Dominance (30%/25% labor threshold)**
- 120-day transaction hold for cost-and-market-impact review
- 10% of Illinois annual revenue penalty per finding
- Mandatory remediation plan with public 60-day response requirement
- Goes beyond any current US state framework. Massachusetts healthcare model reaches 215 days but sector-specific. Trade Tier B to protect Tier A.

## TIER 2 — TRADE IF NECESSARY
*Valuable. Defensible. But expendable if Tier 1 trades don't satisfy opponents.*

**Section 8A — Tier A Advisory Findings (40%/30% labor threshold)**
- Division advisory findings on abuse conduct published publicly
- No penalty at Tier A — findings only
- This is the least aggressive piece of 8A. Still novel. Still citable as precedent once published. Trade only if needed to protect Section 3 and Section 4.

**Section 4 — Tier C Enhanced Declarations (Year 5, 25% threshold)**
- Mandatory public findings on all Tier C dominant entity declarations
- Private right of action trigger in enforcement
- Division preliminary structural remedy petition authority
- Trade Year 5 escalation to protect Year 1 and Year 3 escalation.

**Section 5 — Dominance Harm Treble Damages**
- Treble damages specifically for Tier C dominance harm
- Class action authorization for dominance harm
- Trade the multiplier. Keep the private right of action at single damages.

## TIER 2B — PROACTIVE CONCESSION OFFERINGS
*Offer these proactively to give opponents something to take credit for. Protects the floor by making compliance feel achievable rather than punitive. Do not wait for opponents to demand these — offer them as good faith concessions early in negotiation.*

**Verified Competitive Dominance Designation**
- Good-faith dominant entities with three-year clean record can petition for reduced declaration burden
- Joint Hunter Auditor and human citizen panel assessment
- Designation reduces Tier B operational friction without eliminating transparency
- Offering this proactively signals that the law distinguishes good actors from bad actors — weakens the "punishes success" attack
- Trade the designation timeline or assessment criteria if needed. Do not trade the concept — it is the good-actor off-ramp that makes the dominance framework politically sustainable.

**Competitive Component Confidentiality Window Extension**
- For entities with confirmed dominance finding, competitive component extended to execution plus 24 hours
- Reduces strategic exposure to competitors monitoring declarations
- Offer this proactively to dominant entities engaging in good faith. It costs nothing substantive — declarations remain fully public — but reduces the most immediate operational pain point for compliant entities.

## TIER 3 — HOLD AS LONG AS POSSIBLE
*These look aggressive but are more defensible than they appear. Hold these through negotiation — trade only under significant pressure.*

**Section 4 — Tier B 120-Day Transaction Hold**
- 120-day hold for cost-and-market-impact review of Tier B dominant entities
- Massachusetts healthcare precedent exists at 215 days but sector-specific
- Negotiate down to 60 days if necessary. Do not accept less than 30 days — that's already in the healthcare framework.

**Section 4 — Year 5 Declaration Window (30 days Tier 1)**
- 30-day advance declaration window for Tier 1 entities at Year 5
- Automatic escalation from 7 days at Phase 2 launch
- Negotiate the speed of escalation if necessary. Do not negotiate the final destination.

**Section 3 — Tier B Market Dominance Threshold (30% product markets)**
- 30% threshold is New York's labor market standard applied broadly to product markets
- Goes beyond current US precedent for product markets
- Negotiate to retain 30% for labor markets only if product market application is challenged. Labor market dominance at 30% is the more defensible position.

**Section 16 — Interstate Compact Accountability Mechanism**
- Director must explain publicly if compact negotiations haven't started within 24 months
- Citizen oversight board review of explanation
- Soft accountability only — no hard consequence. Still draws opposition from entities that don't want compact expansion. Trade the timeline, not the mechanism.

## WHAT IS NOT SACRIFICIAL — THE FLOOR

These do not appear above because they are not tradeable. If an opponent gets these, the law is gutted.

- **Section 2 Fifth Principle** — market position is not a license. One sentence. Survives everything.
- **Section 3 Tier A market dominance definition** — 40% product / 30% labor. Citable precedent. Non-negotiable.
- **Section 3 private equity entity and upstream control definitions** — closes the holding company loophole. Non-negotiable.
- **Section 3 and Section 8A market share aggregation — related entities** — fragmentation of market share across subsidiaries under common upstream control does not reduce dominance exposure. Closes the shell company threshold evasion gap. Non-negotiable. Loss of this provision guts the upstream control framework entirely.
- **Section 4 evasion restructuring provision** — restructuring within 24 months of dominance finding triggers automatic review. Paired with aggregation — both must survive together. Non-negotiable.
- **Section 4 Tier A enhanced declarations** — dominant entities declare more, earlier. No violation required. This is the operative floor of the dominance framework.
- **Section 4 second-request authority** — Division can pause a transaction to investigate. 30-day hold minimum. Non-negotiable below that floor.
- **Section 4 declaration window escalation** — automatic, no legislative vote. The ratchet itself is non-negotiable.
- **The void mechanism** — undeclared transactions disappear. Not fined. Gone. This is the sharpest tooth. Non-negotiable.
- **The floor fund structural protection** — three-fifths majority plus referendum to raid it. Non-negotiable.
- **Section 8 constitutional elevation** — the self-defense mechanism. Non-negotiable.
- **Whistleblower mandatory anonymity** — non-negotiable.
- **Section 16 UAPNA interstate compact intent** — the named states and the model act. Non-negotiable. The accountability mechanism timing is negotiable. The intent is not.
- **Section 8A Division rulemaking authority** — Division must publish conduct guidance within 180 days. Answers the vagueness attack. Non-negotiable.
- **Section 8A Parker doctrine active supervision statement** — protects the entire Section 8A regime from federal preemption challenge. Non-negotiable.

## NEGOTIATING LOGIC

When an opponent attacks Section 8A Tier C — let them win it. You've already won the definition in Section 3 and the enhanced declarations in Section 4. The label is gone. The substance survives.

When an opponent attacks the 25% threshold — negotiate to 30%. You've already won 40% in the same provision. They think they won. You kept the ratchet.

When an opponent attacks the transaction hold length — negotiate from 120 days to 60. You've already won the hold itself. Massachusetts healthcare operates at 60 days. You're still ahead of every existing cross-sector framework.

When an opponent attacks the "punishes success" framing — offer the Verified Competitive Dominance designation proactively. You give them a good-actor win. You keep the dominance framework intact.

The strategy is: give them the number, keep the mechanism. Give them the ceiling, keep the floor. Give them the label, keep the definition. Give them the concession, keep the ratchet.
