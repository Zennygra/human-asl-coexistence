**What this bill does in one sentence:**
It requires major economic actors to publicly disclose significant transactions before they execute — so Illinois residents, small businesses, and legislators see what's coming instead of finding out after the fact.

---

**The problem it solves:**
Right now, the most consequential economic decisions affecting Illinois workers, communities, and markets happen invisibly. A corporation announces mass layoffs after the decision is final. A foreign sovereign wealth fund acquires Illinois infrastructure before anyone knew it was for sale. Automated trading systems move markets before any human can respond. The people most affected find out last.

**The solution:**
A tiered public declaration system. The bigger you are, the more advance notice you give. Tier 1 entities — those doing over $1 billion in Illinois annually — give 30 days notice at full implementation. Small businesses give 72 hours. Everyone sees what the biggest players are doing before their own window opens. That's the information leveling mechanism.

---

**What it is NOT:**
- Not a regulation of business activity — entities can proceed with any transaction after declaring it
- Not a regulation of artificial intelligence as a technology
- Not a partisan document — applies equally to all entities regardless of political affiliation or home state
- Not an unfunded mandate — funded by transaction fees from the entities subject to it

---

**Key protections built in:**
- Penalties go directly to communities and an independently protected floor fund — not to general state revenue
- Anti-capture provisions prevent corporations from buying the experts who would oppose the bill
- Political neutrality statement prevents partisan capture from either direction
- Constitutional elevation triggers if the bill faces sustained legal attack or legislative tampering
